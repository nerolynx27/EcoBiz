# EcoBiz Business Model Canvas

**Status:** Founder-approved strategic direction; discovery-stage business model  
**Prepared:** 27 September 2026  
**Location:** Universiti Tenaga Nasional (UNITEN), Malaysia  
**Purpose:** Central business strategy reference for the team, university stakeholders, prospective partners, and competition mentors.

This document reflects the founder interview and the approved strategic summary. It describes intended business operations, not a software development specification. Approval of this strategy does not establish customer demand, partner commitments, institutional permission, product suitability, or financial viability.

**Status conventions:** **Confirmed decision** means a founder-approved intention or scope choice. **Proposal** means an arrangement to negotiate or test. **Hypothesis** means an expectation without sufficient evidence. **Outstanding** means a decision or validation task remains open. Reported observations and estimates are identified separately.

## 1. Executive Overview

EcoBiz is a student-led venture seeking to reduce food waste at UNITEN through two connected activities: selling edible surplus meals at discounted prices and developing a process to recover suitable organic waste. Its intended benefits are more affordable meals for students, additional revenue for vendors, and productive use of materials that would otherwise be discarded.

The immediate priority is a university startup competition. The team intends to present completed meal purchases, a functioning and polished application demonstrated live, a documented fertilizer experiment, and physical packaging. The initial meal pilot targets a cabin vendor within the founder's residential block. Participation has not yet been secured.

Both business phases are essential to EcoBiz's long-term purpose, but their competition evidence will differ. The meal pilot aims for real transactions. The fertilizer demonstration will be an experimental sample made from purchased materials, not a product offered for immediate sale. It will not establish that actual campus waste has been successfully processed.

The team has approximately RM100 in pooled personal funding. The proposed meal revenue is a fixed RM0.50 service fee per completed sale. Future fertilizer sales are intended, but production economics, product readiness, buyer demand, and pricing remain unvalidated. The team intends to continue after the competition if practical and to discuss support with UNITEN administration.

## 2. Project Identity

### Vision

A UNITEN community where food is valued and less goes to waste.

### Mission

Make surplus meals more affordable and develop responsible ways to recover suitable organic waste through a student-led, financially sustainable business.

### Core values

- **Gratitude through action:** Treat food as a valuable resource and express gratitude through practical efforts to reduce waste.
- **Affordability:** Make participating students' meal spending more manageable while preserving a worthwhile offer for vendors.
- **Responsible resource use:** Prioritize edible food rescue and establish which remaining materials can be responsibly recovered.
- **Honest evidence:** Distinguish intentions, experiments, observed results, and verified outcomes in all business claims.
- **Community contribution:** Build reciprocal relationships within UNITEN and consider sharing future profits through donations when financially feasible.

### Strategic positioning

EcoBiz is intended as a campus-focused food rescue and organic waste recovery business, starting with a small, directly managed vendor relationship. The application serves as a business channel connecting customers, vendors, and operational records.

The founder's motivation includes concern for people experiencing hunger, including in Syria, Lebanon, and Palestine. EcoBiz's direct initial contribution is local food-waste reduction and affordability; it does not claim to directly alleviate hunger in those locations. Donations from future profits are an aspiration, with no committed percentage, recipient, or schedule.

## 4. Proposed Solution

### Phase 1: Surplus food marketplace

Participating vendors offer available surplus meals at discounted prices. Students reserve, collect directly from the vendor within a defined late-evening window, and pay at collection. The vendor confirms completed purchases and periodically settles EcoBiz's service fees.

The initial channel is a private Telegram group promoted through the residence's main WhatsApp group. Once the application is ready for use, reservations move to it and Telegram remains a promotional channel. The preference is for photo-based offers showing a short meal description, price, and available quantity.

### Phase 2: Organic waste recovery

The intended future operation provides vendors with a large plastic container for suitable organic waste, followed by next-day collection by the EcoBiz team. Faiz is the likely collection and processing lead, assisted by teammates. The processing method, acceptable inputs, storage conditions, processing site, capacity, and product validation are outstanding.

For the competition, the team will document an experiment using purchased materials and present an experimental sample with packaging. The planned fermentation mentioned in the initial project notes is not a confirmed method or a validated production timeline.

### Relationship between the phases

The intended sequence prioritizes selling edible surplus before considering suitable residual material for recovery. Uncollected meals are candidates for recovery, not automatically acceptable processing inputs. EcoBiz's long-term ambition connects the phases, while the competition demonstrations remain explicitly separate until actual campus-waste processing is demonstrated.

## 6. Competitive Differentiation

EcoBiz's intended differentiation is the connection between student affordability, vendor revenue recovery, and downstream organic waste recovery within one campus community. A nearby initial vendor and residence-based outreach may allow close coordination and quick feedback.

These are positioning choices, not established competitive advantages. No competitor review or independent market validation has been completed for this strategy. EcoBiz must not claim to be unique, the first campus solution, or a zero-waste operation without supporting evidence.

Validation should examine existing vendor discounting, worker redistribution, informal student purchasing groups, campus sustainability initiatives, and alternative waste arrangements. EcoBiz must establish whether its additional coordination produces benefits beyond these existing practices.

## 7. Business Operations

### Meal pilot

1. Interview the cabin vendor about surplus quantities, current leftover uses, suitable meals, pricing, operating burden, and participation conditions.
2. Agree the offer, responsibilities, collection window, payment confirmation, cancellation handling, and fee settlement before live sales.
3. Promote available meals through the initial Telegram channel and later the application, displaying the total price payable by the student.
4. Students reserve without advance payment. The vendor sets aside the agreed quantity; packing arrangements should be agreed with the vendor.
5. Students collect directly between **9:40 and 9:55 pm** and pay the vendor. The founder reports that the vendor normally closes around **10 pm**; the window is intended to avoid delaying workers.
6. The vendor confirms collection and successful payment. Reservations alone do not generate a payable EcoBiz fee.
7. Reconcile completed sales, adjustments, and fees daily in the first launch week, then move to weekly settlement once the process works reliably and the vendor agrees.
8. Record missed collections and follow up according to an agreed policy. With payment at collection, a no-show has paid nothing to retain or refund. No penalty has been approved.

The founder intends to use phone contact for collection-related follow-up. Requesting a number at the first reservation was proposed; timing, access, retention, and responsibility for contacting customers remain to be settled. A contact number does not itself establish a no-show penalty or an obligation to pay.

### Waste and experimental work

The proposed future collection model uses vendor-side plastic containers and next-day team collection. Before operation, the team needs to agree storage location, separation of acceptable inputs, cleaning responsibilities, transport, processing location, and handling of rejected material. Until these are resolved, the collection model remains a proposal.

The competition experiment uses purchased materials. Its record should identify inputs, quantities, method, dates, observations, costs, and limitations. Packaging must identify the sample as experimental. A visually finished package is not evidence of product effectiveness or sale readiness.

## 8. Revenue and Financial Sustainability

### Meal revenue

The preferred proposal adds **RM0.50 per completed meal sale** to the vendor's discounted price. This is a fixed service fee, not a percentage commission or a service tax.

| Item | Illustrative transaction |
|---|---:|
| Vendor's discounted meal price | RM7.00 |
| EcoBiz service fee | RM0.50 |
| Total student payment to vendor at collection | RM7.50 |
| Vendor amount before its own costs | RM7.00 |
| Amount payable by vendor to EcoBiz | RM0.50 |

The founder estimates ordinary rice-and-chicken or rice-and-fish meals at **RM8–10**, with a possible discounted vendor price of **RM5–7**, before EcoBiz's fee. These are interview-stage estimates, not verified prices or vendor commitments.

**Gross meal fee revenue = confirmed completed meal sales × RM0.50**, adjusted for applicable cancellations, refunds, or corrections. This is revenue, not profit. Payment disputes, refund responsibilities, and settlement evidence require agreement with the vendor.

### Fertilizer revenue

The tentative future price is **around RM15**, with package size unspecified. The final price must follow measurement of production, packaging, handling, collection, and other applicable costs, along with prospective buyer interviews. The competition sample is experimental and is not intended for immediate sale; no fertilizer revenue should be assumed for that demonstration.

### Funding and cost discipline

The available team contribution is **approximately RM100**, self-funded. No allocation or outside funding is confirmed. Before purchasing materials, the team should price essential trial inputs, containers, packaging, and transport and fit commitments to the available funds or revise scope. Biodegradable packaging appears in the initial plans, but sourcing, cost, and any environmental claims remain to be verified.

Pilot records should capture cash expenses and team time separately. Volunteer effort may keep immediate spending low but does not demonstrate that ongoing operations are economically sustainable.

Financial viability requires enough income to cover the recurring cost of serving transactions and handling materials. There is insufficient evidence for a break-even estimate or financial projection. Future donations should be considered from actual available profits after costs and operating needs; no donation obligation is currently established.

## 10. Sustainability and Social Impact

EcoBiz intends to reduce food waste, improve access to affordable meals, recover vendor revenue, and investigate productive use of suitable organic material. These are objectives, not measured achievements.

| Intended outcome | Proposed indicator | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Successful meal rescue | Number of paid, collected surplus meals; measured meal weight where practical. | Reservations are not completed rescue. Confirm what would otherwise have happened to the food. |
| Student affordability | Difference between the ordinary price of a comparable meal and the student's total paid price, including the fee. | Use verified comparable prices rather than assumed savings. |
| Vendor benefit | Revenue from completed pilot sales, vendor feedback, additional work, and willingness to continue. | Revenue is not profit; consider costs and displacement of existing sales or worker meals. |
| Reliable operations | No-show rate, completed collections within the window, and settlement discrepancies. | A small pilot does not establish campus-wide performance. |
| Organic waste recovery | Weight collected, accepted for processing, rejected, and successfully processed, tracked separately. | Purchased experimental inputs are not campus waste diverted. Collection alone is not successful recovery. |
| Experimental progress | Documented method, inputs, observations, output, and any suitable external assessment. | A sample or completed processing period does not prove fertilizer effectiveness. |
| Community contribution | Actual donations, if later made, with amounts and recipients recorded. | An intention to donate is not an achieved social impact. |

No greenhouse-gas reduction, landfill diversion, fertilizer performance, or donation results have been established. Environmental claims should account for the actual alternative destination of materials and the resources used in collection, processing, and packaging. The initial rice-to-birds observation must not be relabeled as landfill diversion.
